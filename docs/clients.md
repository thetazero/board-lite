# Playing on a TV and phones

The shared board runs in a browser on the TV; each player joins from their phone's browser with a room code. One machine runs the server, which hosts the game; the TV and phones only need a browser on the same network.

It's one game whatever the client: phones, the TV and the command line all use the same room interface, so a table can mix phone players, CLI players, Claude players and bots. See "Playing from the command line" below, and [`play-vs-claude.md`](play-vs-claude.md) to play against Claude.

## Run it

Needs Node 22.18 or newer. In the repo:

```
npm install
npm run serve                  # or: node src/server/server.ts --port 8080
```

It prints the addresses to use, e.g.

```
board-lite is running on port 8080.
  TV:     http://localhost:8080/tv   (or http://192.168.1.20:8080/tv)
  Phones: http://192.168.1.20:8080/
  Phones over Tailscale: http://100.101.102.103:8080/
```

1. **TV:** open the TV address in a browser on the TV (or on a laptop plugged into it), full screen (F11). It makes a room and shows a QR code of the room's address, the address itself and the room's 4-letter code. Each seat appears as a place card with its tower, and the towers stand in the picture at the top in the players' colours. The server's terminal prints, for each new room, the commands that seat a CLI player or Claude players at it (the TV doesn't show them).
2. **Phones:** point the phone's camera at the TV's QR code and tap the link (or type the address the TV shows, `http://<that machine's address>:8080/ABCD`), type a name, and tap **Join**: two taps and a name, and the name is remembered for next time. (The bare address works too; then type the code.) Each player gets a colour.
3. **Start:** with 2 to 6 players in, press **Start game** on the TV or on the first player's phone. **Add a bot** fills an empty seat with the random test bot (handy for trying it alone); bots get clockwork names (Tick, Gizmo, ...) so they never share a name with a person. On the TV, a seat's × (shown on hover) removes it before the start.

If phones can't connect: they must be on the same Wi-Fi as the server machine, or on the same tailnet using its Tailscale address, and its firewall must allow the port (on Linux with ufw: `sudo ufw allow 8080/tcp`, or `sudo ufw allow in on tailscale0` for the tailnet). Some guest Wi-Fi networks block devices from reaching each other.

A phone that reloads, closes its tab, sleeps or drops off the Wi-Fi gets its seat back on its own when the page is open again (the seat is remembered in that browser; coming back into view or back online reconnects at once). On another phone, or a browser that forgot it, the room's address mid-game lists the seats nobody is at: tap your name to take yours back. The TV shows the address only in the lobby, since nobody new joins a game that has started; the phones' address is still printed in the server's terminal.

Nobody stalls the table:

- **Quit:** the **⋯** next to your name on the phone opens **Quit game**. A bot plays your seat for the rest of the game, and the TV says so. To come back, open the room's address and tap your name (anyone can take a seat that was quit the same way). If every player quits, the game ends: the bots stop, the TV says so, and the server forgets the room after 10 minutes unless someone comes back.
- **Away:** when the table waits on someone whose phone is gone (closed, asleep, out of range), everyone else's phone shows "Ada is away." with **Let a bot play**, a minute after their phone went. The bot plays for them until their phone is back, which hands the seat back at once.

The bot that plays for someone does as little as it can (`standInMove` in `src/bot.ts`): it declines offers, ends the turn, keeps the first building card, votes for the first target, sends no dragons and discards the oldest cards. It changes no rule: it only makes the moves the player would have to make for the game to go on. A TV that reloads keeps its room (the code is in its address). The server saves each game to `~/.cache/board-lite/rooms/<code>.json` after every change, and who sits where beside it (`<code>.seats.json`). After a restart, `npm run serve` hosts every room from the last 6 hours again under its own code: the TV and the phones reconnect on their own, each player to their own seat. A phone whose room the server no longer has goes back to the join form and says so. `npm run serve -- --game <file>` hosts one saved game: one the server hosted before keeps its code and seats; one with no seats file beside it has every seat open for players to claim by joining.

## Playing with no TV

No TV, or players who aren't in the same room: on the join form (the server's bare address, `http://<that machine's address>:8080/`), type your name and tap **New game** under "No TV". Your phone makes the room and you're its first player. The lobby shows the room's QR code, its address and its code, for the others to join from their phones; **Add a bot** and **Start game** are yours, as when there's a TV. One phone and a few bots is a whole game (handy for trying things on a train).

Each phone then shows the table as well as its own hand:

- **You / Table**, by the talk bar at the bottom: one tap switches between your own view and the table, any time. The table is what the TV would show (the board, every player's card, the feed, the titles and beasts, the raid's vote and commitments), one thing under another, to scroll through. Table talk stays on screen in both.
- **Cutscenes cut in on their own:** when the TV would play one of its moments (the round's production rolls, a raid's fight or "No one raids", any fight against a beast), every phone switches to the table for it and back to where it was when it's done. Switch by hand during one and the phone stays where you put it. The end of the game is on your own view already (the winner and the standings), and on the table too.
- **Light:** the table is the TV's page with the flat map, never the 3D board: a phone doesn't load three.js.

A game made on a TV is the same as before: no switch, no table on the phones.

## Playing from the command line

A CLI player sits at the same table: they join with the room's address and a name, and use the seat token `join` gives them in every command after:

```
node src/cli.ts join http://192.168.1.20:8080/ABCD Claude    # prints your seat and token
node src/cli.ts view http://192.168.1.20:8080/ABCD <token>
node src/cli.ts do   http://192.168.1.20:8080/ABCD <token> build c12 2,-1
node src/cli.ts wait http://192.168.1.20:8080/ABCD <token>
```

Everything else is in [`cli.md`](cli.md). The CLI gets the same JSON a phone does and prints it as text. The TV and phones update the moment a CLI player moves, and the other way round. If the first player to join is at the CLI, `node src/cli.ts start <room> <token>` starts the game.

## What's where

- **TV** (everything public): the board in 3D, in folded paper: each hex a creased sheet of coloured paper in an origami tray, seen from a little off straight down, every building a folded paper model on its owner's coloured plate (it pops up off the page when it's built; an upgrade adds a gold rim and pennant), and every dragon in origami at home on its owner's tower, roost or hatchery, flying a lap round its home now and then (eggs sit in nests; hungry or hurt dragons lie down and stay in). The board is the TV: it fills the screen's height, centred, and everything else stands in the columns either side of it and in its corners. A card per player, the first half of the table on the left and the rest on the right, sized to be read from the couch (as large as the player count allows, stepping down only to fit): their points, cards in hand, what they're doing when the table waits on them (taking a turn, answering an offer, discarding, voting…), the titles they hold, and their dragons by name (a second of the same kind is "II") with life stage, growth, combat power (a gold shield and a number), and health or why they're sitting out the round (hungry, or healing after a fight, with the rounds until it can raid again). No resources and no income: those are each player's own, on their phone. Open trade offers sit over the top edge of the board, on its tray. In the bottom-left corner, the last few events and table talk in one feed, the events drawn as icon rows rather than sentences (who, then what they built, hatched, traded or played); in the bottom-right, the titles (everyone's count, the holder's ringed, and the count it takes to hold one). Each round opens with its number and everyone's production roll, each player's dice tumbling a moment after the last's and landing for the whole table to see (only the dice: not what they bring in). The raid takes over the screen, drawn rather than written: each kingdom's combat power and its defenders as a picture per kind ("×4") with their health, attack and the elements they take double or half from; then an arena, the raiders on the left (each player's dragons that could go, the ones sent lit with their combat power added up, a roost for anyone staying home, a tick once they're ready) and the kingdom on the right, through commitments and fight-or-bail (who has chosen, not what); then the fight replayed blow by blow, a stroke from each attacker to what it hits, health bars falling as the hits land, whoever bailed shown flying home, and who won and what they took. When a War Horn adds raids, a dim mark for each one still to come sits by the raid's title. Every fight gets that replay: clearing fauna too, a wolf pack as much as a great beast, with the player's dragons on the left, the beast on the right, then whether it falls and what they got. At the end the winner gets their moment (their tower and dragons under paper confetti) above the final standings: place, points (with any that were secret cards), ties marked, and how the winner won a tie if they did. The phone shows the same in place of the game: no resources or income left over.
- **Phone** (only yours): your resources, each with what a round brings in under it (production minus what your dragons eat, e.g. `+2 +3d6`), your points including secret ones, your roost (your dragons, their growth, combat power, health and whether they're hungry or healing, and an empty nest for each dragon you have room for; tap a dragon to see its card full size, its stats at every life stage with the one it's at now marked, or leaf through your other dragons from there), on every screen of the game, your hand (illustrated paper cards held in a fan along the bottom edge; on your turn the ones you can play now glow; tap one to see it full size with its rules and play it, or leaf through the hand from there by swiping or with ‹ ›; press a card and slide along the fan and the card under your thumb rises, straight and big enough to read, which is how a full, overlapping hand is read; hold a card still to open it; drag one up out of the fan and let go to play it, straight away or on to the choice it needs (the hex, the dragon), or, if it can't be played now, to open it and see why; new cards fly in as you draw them; to discard, tap cards in the fan (or drag them up): each one picked is laid out face up above **Discard**, with an empty place for each still to pick, and tapping it there puts it back; while discarding, hold a card to read it, with **Discard** or **Don't discard** under it; to offer cards in a trade, tap or drag them the same way), and whatever you're being asked right now. At the start of each round: keep one of the two building cards you drew. On your turn: play cards (pick the hex on a small copy of the map; for a building that counts its neighbours, each hex shows its count), basic buildings with no card, the egg special project, the bank, trades and **End turn**. Out of turn: offer the acting player a trade, and accept or decline offers made to you. In the raid, with your roost and hand still on screen (the hand gives way on a short screen) and the kingdom you'd fight drawn above your choice: vote (each kingdom with its combat power), commit dragons (each with its power, the total on the button) and lock in, fight or bail in secret (seeing who else is going, and with how much power).

- **Scenes: one moment at a time.** A round's opening roll, a fight's replay (a raid, or clearing fauna) and a raid no one joined are scenes: the whole table watches them on the TV. While one plays, every phone says "Watch the TV" (and shows nothing that changed, so the ending isn't given away), bots and CLI players wait, and the TV's panels, feed and titles stay as they were when the scene began; when it ends, the next decision comes up everywhere at once. A raid that no one could have joined (no dragon was raid-ready, as in round 1) is no scene. In a game with no TV (remote mode), each phone's table view plays the scene and cuts to it, the same hold applies, and a phone switched back to You says "Watch the table". With no screen watching at all (CLI or playtest games), nothing waits. The scenes and their lengths are in `web/scenes.ts`, which the TV and the server both read.

- **Table talk**: people in the room just talk, but players who aren't (Claude, or someone at a terminal) only hear what goes through the game. The bar at the bottom of the phone shows the latest message; tap it to read the conversation and to talk to everyone or privately to one player. The TV shows public talk in its feed of events, as it happens. CLI players use `say` and `tell`.

## Trying it without a group

```
node src/server/demo.ts --players 3 --round 3
```

starts a server with a game already in round 3, played by bots except for the first seat, and prints a TV link and a phone link for that seat (open the phone link in a narrow browser window). `--all-bots` lets bots play every seat; `--seed N` picks the game.

## Staged screenshots (for look tuning)

```
npm run shots                    # every scene, under a minute and a half
npm run shots -- --only raid     # scenes whose name contains "raid"
npm run shots -- --every-size    # every shot at every screen size, for a full audit
npm run shots -- --out DIR       # somewhere else
```

writes screenshots of the TV and phones in staged situations to `~/.cache/board-lite/staged-shots/latest/`, with an `index.html` contact sheet. The run before moves to `latest.previous`; files that differ from it are starred in the output and outlined in the sheet, so a visual change shows exactly which screens it touched.

- **Screens:** the TV at 1920×1080, the phone at 390×844 (2×). Shots marked `sizes` are also taken at the other sizes: the TV at 1280×720 (`@720p`), the phone at 360×800 (`@360`), 320×568 (`@320`), sideways at 844×390 (`@landscape`) and on a tablet at 820×1180 (`@tablet`). `--every-size` does that for every shot.
- **A phone shot is what the phone's screen shows.** A page longer than the screen is shot one screen at a time, scrolling down (`name.png`, `name.2.png`, ... up to 4), so the hand, the talk bar and an open card sit where they sit on a real phone. Don't shoot the whole page at once: Chrome stretches the screen to the page's height for that, and everything sized against the screen grows with it.
- **The scenes** (`src/shots/scenes.ts`): each is a seeded bot game played until a condition holds, plus a few scripted commands (bots don't offer trades or talk), or a lobby. A shot can also open the page as a device that remembers a name, type into the join form, open the TV without its host token, drop the connection, stop the page before its script loads, or have something happen at the table after the page is up (`then`). Add a scene by adding an entry there. What they cover:
  - **Joining and leaving:** the TV lobby empty, with one player (Start disabled), with three, and full (six, long names, bots, someone away, a Tailscale address); the phone lobby as the first player (alone, and with others) and as anyone else; the join form from the room's address, without a code, and remembering a name; every join error (no such room, name taken, blank name, full); removed from the lobby, Leave, and Join another game; a saved game hosted again (open seats) and a phone taking a seat in it; the room's address mid-game with everyone at their seat.
  - **Away and quitting** (`away-3p`): a phone seeing someone away (with **Let a bot play**), the TV once a bot plays for them, the phone's menu with **Quit game**, the phone after quitting, the TV once someone quits, the room's address listing the seats to take back, a seat taken back, and the TV when an away player is back.
  - **Connecting:** both clients before their script loads, and with the connection dropped ("Reconnecting…"); an error toast on each (a second TV screen tapping Start, accepting an offer the offerer can no longer pay).
  - **The turn:** the opening round card, each player's production roll coming up in turn and all of them landed, early and late boards for 2 to 6 players, the 3D board frame by frame (a building popping up, a dragon's flight), a port on the coast, and every basic building and both towers side by side (the TV, and the phone's basic buildings and its map), the eleven production cards each by its class's basic building (the TV, the phone's map and a hand of them), the dragon, trade and monument cards with each upgrade on a building it fits (the TV, the phone's map and a hand of them), six players with long names, bots and someone away; the phone on its turn and off it, with an empty hand, a hand of every card, a hand of playable cards (Secret Hoard kept, Growth Elixir and Healing Potion choosing a dragon, Monument, an upgrade), every reason a card can't be played, a card you can't afford, a card tapped out of turn; choosing a hex to build a card, a basic building and an upgrade; the bank with picks made; trade offers in, out (on your turn and someone else's), being composed, and the trade tab out of turn.
  - **Discarding:** the TV, one card picked, several picked (laid out above the button), a thumb sliding along the hand to read a card, and waiting for others. The hand also has a thumb on it (`phone-hand-slide`) and a card dragged up to play (`phone-hand-pull`); a shot's `press` puts a finger down and slides it without letting go.
  - **The raid:** two raids in one round, the vote (voting and voted), commit (one tapped, nothing yet, locked in, no dragon able to raid), fight or bail (choosing, chosen, not in it), the fight's replay (mid-way and at its end, one a raider bailed out of, and a phone saying "Watch the TV" while it plays) and a win, a loss, everyone bailing, nobody committing, and the next round's production banner right after a raid's.
  - **Fauna:** the beasts on the board, and the phone choosing one and the dragons to send; a fight against a Cave Bear (mid-replay and at its end, `tv-fauna-fight-3p`, `tv-fauna-cleared-3p`) and against the Thunderbird (`tv-beast-fight-3p`, `tv-beast-slain-3p`).
  - **Table talk:** the TV's talk panel, the phone's talk bar with unread messages, and the talk sheet open with a private message.
  - **The end:** final scores for 4 and 6 players, the winner's and everyone else's phone.
  - **No TV** (`remote-*`, the table view also shot on its own as `tv-remote-*`): the lobby on the phone that made the game, your own view with the You / Table switch, the table view switched to (3 and 6 players) and with the talk sheet open, the vote and the commitments on the table, the round's production rolls, a raid's fight and a fight against the Thunderbird cutting in on a phone, a fight's end and the Thunderbird slain, all 6 production rolls landed, and the end on both views.
  - **Not shot:** "You are not in this game." (the phone shows it if its seat stops being a player's mid-game, which nothing in the server does), "Someone else has your seat now." (a phone that was away when someone took its seat by name), "… is playing on another phone." (taking a seat by name while its phone is there; the seat list doesn't offer those) and the browser's own bubble for an empty name (the form's `required`; a name of only spaces reaches the server's "Pick a name.").
- **The hand never covers the page.** After a run, the output lists every phone screen where the hand of cards sits over the page's text or buttons, and what it covers. It should list none: a page too long for the hand to fit below it puts the hand after the page instead (`fitHand` in `web/phone.ts`).
- **The same every run:** the games are seeded, the pages' clock is frozen, CSS animations are off, bots never move, and the TV shows a fixed address, so the same code gives byte-identical images. A change to the rules or the numbers can move a scene to a different moment (or the next seed, if its condition stops holding); `npm test` checks every scene still stages.
- **How:** the real server and clients, with each scene hosted as a room whose seats nobody plays, so nothing moves on its own; one headless Chrome (about 1 GB) driven over the DevTools protocol on a pipe, no extra dependencies. Chrome quits when the run ends, however it ends (Ctrl-C, `kill -9`, its terminal closing), and the next run clears away a killed run's profile. The 3D board renders on the CPU (SwiftShader). Its clock (`window.boardClock`) starts frozen at 0; a shot with `frames` takes one picture per board-clock time, which is how `tv-build-3p-*` shows a building popping up and `tv-flight-2p-*` a dragon's flight. It finds Chrome at the usual paths, or set `CHROME=/path/to/chrome`. Its profile and the output stay under `~/.cache/board-lite/`, never `/tmp`.

## How it's built

- `src/server/server.ts`: one `node:http` server. It serves `web/`, three.js for the TV's 3D board (from `node_modules`, at `/vendor/three/`), and the room API (create, join, add bot, start, leave, quit, a bot for someone away, do a command, plus text `view`/`wait`/`status` for the CLI) and pushes a fresh snapshot to every TV and phone over server-sent events whenever anything changes. The browser code is TypeScript; the server strips its types as it serves it (Node's `stripTypeScriptTypes`), so there's no build step here either.
- `src/server/room.ts`: a room's game, its seats (each player, phone or CLI, holds a secret token for its seat), and the bots. Every decision goes through the engine's `apply`.
- `src/state.ts`: the JSON each client gets. Every log line carries its event as data (`GameEvent` in `src/engine.ts`) next to its text; the TV and phones draw the event, and the CLI and Claude players print the text (`src/view.ts` turns a snapshot into text). The TV's part holds only public state; a phone's part adds that one player's resources, hand and private events. Nothing else about other players is ever sent to a phone.
- `web/`: `tv.ts` (with `?remote`, the table view inside a phone in a game with no TV: the flat map, laid out for a phone, and a message to the phone when a cutscene starts and ends), `phone.ts`, the TV's 3D board `board3d.ts` (three.js, loaded only on a real TV; the TV falls back to the flat map without WebGL), the shared `common.ts` (the phone's flat map, connection, the final standings), `qr.ts` (the TV's QR code of the room's address, no dependencies; `test/qr.test.ts` checks it against a reference encoder), `icons.ts` (the game's one icon set, folded-paper style: resources, points, cards, elements, and the tower and basic buildings), `art.ts` (the card pictures, and the lobby's and game over's scenes: folded-paper subjects on layered cut paper, inline SVG, nothing to download) and `style.css`. `npm run typecheck` checks them too.
